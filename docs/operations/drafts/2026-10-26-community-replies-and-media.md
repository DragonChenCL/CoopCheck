> Before spending money, separate “keeping chickens” from “selling eggs.” The rules can be different: local animal ordinance (hen/rooster limits), zoning and coop setbacks, accessory-building permits, and state/county requirements for selling eggs at a farmers market. “Chickens allowed” does not automatically mean a coop can go anywhere or eggs can be sold.

**Reusable source-first reply template** (use only when a matching question appears):

> I would verify the current municipal animal code and the zoning/building rules separately. Look for the hen/rooster limit, permit or licensing requirements, the exact setback reference point (property line versus neighboring dwelling), and any separate accessory-structure rules. Forum summaries are useful leads, but they can be outdated or apply only to a particular zoning district. If you share the city and county, the answer can be checked against the current official source rather than guessed.

**Verified city-specific example for a future Sacramento question only:** the City of Sacramento's official poultry page states a maximum of three chickens per residential parcel, no roosters, an annual poultry license and coop permit, rear-yard placement, a 15–42 sq ft coop, and at least 20 ft from the nearest neighboring dwelling. Source: https://www.cityofsacramento.gov/community-development/animal-care/pet-licenses/poultry-license. Do not generalize these Sacramento rules to another city.
