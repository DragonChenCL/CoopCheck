03 / HOW TO READ THE NUMBERS

A fair test has to show its assumptions.

OSU Extension recommends at least 3 sq ft of indoor area per bird with regular access to a run/outdoors, versus 8–10 sq ft without outdoor access. We display the low end (8) for the second scenario.

These are general husbandry planning references, not a federal rule or proof that a particular model is appropriate. More space, different breeds and climates may require different arrangements.

Read the original OSU Extension guide ↗
01

Separate sleeping house from run

A whole-unit footprint can be much larger than the enclosed house. Never count the outdoor run as indoor sleeping floor.

02

Don't confuse roosting spots with housing

Perch length, floor, ventilation, nest boxes, access and bird size are different constraints.

03

Unknown means unknown

Where only outer dimensions are shown, contact the seller instead of deriving an invented clear-floor capacity.

04

Check city rules separately

Backyard flock allowances, lot-size requirements and setback distances vary by city and parcel.

04 / MAKE A SMARTER BUYING DECISION

Before you order, ask for these numbers.

✓Clear sleeping-house floor

Actual inside length × width after nests or fixtures, not external roof width.

✓Usable run space

Run dimensions, mesh and cover, included vs sold separately.

✓Real roost length

Linear perch space, placement and suitability for your flock's size.

✓Ventilation & weather

Permanent airflow, drafts, predator barriers and climate suitability.

Resolve a missing dimension before interpreting a coloured result

Use this evidence ladder: a measurement of the clear enclosed floor; a dimensioned drawing explicitly labelled inside; a seller-reported indoor area; then, only as a question to resolve, an overall product footprint. These are different evidence types, not interchangeable confirmations.

A blank or unclear inside measurement should stay unknown. Ask the seller: “What are the clear enclosed sleeping-house dimensions, excluding the wire run and inaccessible fixtures? Does the advertised count describe overnight roosting or a complete housing scenario?” Record their answer, model and date. CoopCheck has not physically measured the three listed products.

A result above one floor-area reference is not a recommendation to purchase. A lower result identifies the size question to resolve; it is not an allegation that the manufacturer broke a law. Use the copied buyer record to keep scenario, measurement source and limitations together.

Try a published-specification case, then challenge the result

Example A: The Omlet Eglu Cube manufacturer comparison publishes a 34 x 33 inch internal width/depth rectangle, or 7.79 sq ft by straightforward arithmetic. Its stated capacity is six large or ten small hens. The rectangle and advertised bird count answer different questions; do not enter 7.79 as independently verified unobstructed floor or treat the manufacturer count as an OSU-equivalent area target. Manufacturer comparison.

Example B: The Producer's Pride Sentinel listing reports 41.74 x 40.35 inches for the coop without its run, but does not say that measurement is clear interior bird floor. Leave the indoor measurement unknown in the checker until the seller supplies it; do not type 11.70 sq ft from the outer envelope as a fact about the usable interior. Seller listing.

What changes the answer? Record whether birds have daily run access. OSU gives at least 3 sq ft per adult bird in the outdoor-access scenario and 8-10 without such access. Neither guide replaces checks of air exchange, roosting access, breed, wet-weather confinement, the local flock limit, or the legitimacy of an advertised claim. OSU source.
