Ten hens: equal run area does not mean equal cost or site fit

Compare square and narrow runs for ten hens using actual perimeter arithmetic. Separate the 40 sq ft indoor target from access and municipal permission.

Once the flock reaches ten, the shape of the outdoor enclosure can change the material quantity even when its area does not change. Work out the geometry before comparing quotes.

Two ways to draw 100 square feet

Use 40 sq ft of clear indoor floor and a separate 100 sq ft run as the site's daily-outdoor-access references for ten adult layers. A 5 x 8 ft clear room supplies the indoor area. Outside, both 10 x 10 ft and 5 x 20 ft rectangles supply 100 square feet, but their perimeter is not the same.

The narrow option adds ten linear feet of boundary and 60 square feet of wall covering before overlaps, gates or waste. These figures compare geometry; they do not specify a safe fence height or structural design.

Do not convert a floor total into a promise that it fits

The two floor targets total 140 square feet, but a yard with 140 square feet of spare land may still fail to fit either arrangement. A strip, a corner, a slope or a required separation zone can prevent usable placement. Draw the actual boundary, house, gate opening and both enclosure rectangles to scale.

Mark any area reserved exclusively for people or permanent equipment separately. Ground that birds can still use is not automatically lost, whereas an inaccessible storage bay should not be counted as run.

Quote the same enclosure specification

Give each supplier the same run width, length and covering height. Ask whether the quote includes roof covering, a digging barrier, gates, fixings and cut-off waste. The mesh calculator separates those quantities; it cannot determine the appropriate predator protection for your site.

If one quote covers only the four walls and another includes the roof, their headline prices are not comparable. Leave unquoted items blank in the cost worksheet rather than treating them as zero.

A ten-hen decision has two independent stop checks

First confirm the local animal allowance for the actual address. A large yard does not establish permission for ten hens. Then check the housing layout: the arithmetic gives three nests after rounding and 7.5 ft of usable perch at the selected ratio. Neither a legal count nor adequate area answers the other question.

Keep the gate and cleaning route on the drawing you take to the city or builder. Do not order a structure from the area total alone.
