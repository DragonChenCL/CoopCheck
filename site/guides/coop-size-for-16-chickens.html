Sixteen hens: plan the maintenance system, not only the room

Check bird floor versus a service aisle, weekly workload and staged expansion for a sixteen-hen flock. Includes clear assumptions and an owner-filled log.

At sixteen birds, a drawing should explain how feed, bedding and waste move through the enclosure. A square-foot total does not tell a keeper whether the daily routine is workable.

When an 8 x 8 room stops being 64 usable square feet

The selected indoor target is 64 sq ft. A clear 8 x 8 ft room reaches it. But a permanently separated 2 x 8 ft service aisle leaves only 48 square feet accessible to birds. That is 16 below the target. A clear 8 x 10 ft room with the same excluded aisle leaves 64.

This subtraction applies only when the aisle is actually unavailable to the birds. Do not subtract a painted walkway that the flock can use normally. Measure permanent partitions and stored items instead of inventing a percentage “safety allowance.”

Map the service route before selecting equipment

Mark where fresh bedding enters, where used bedding leaves and where feed is stored dry and inaccessible to pests. Test door clearance using your actual carrier or wheelbarrow. Make sure the route to four nests does not require moving stored materials. The perch reference totals 12 ft; it is combined usable length, not a recommendation for one unsupported twelve-foot board.

For the separate 160 sq ft run, a 10 x 16 ft usable rectangle is one example. Add the gate swing and any people-only service space to the drawing without counting them as guaranteed bird area.

For illustration only, 15 minutes daily plus a 45-minute weekly task totals 150 minutes, or 2.5 hours. That is arithmetic, not a prediction of how much care sixteen hens require. Time an actual routine or a comparable practice setup before accepting a workload estimate.

Separate an expansion plan from a bird order

Record the number allowed locally, the number the usable floor was assessed for and the number you actually intend to keep. These are three different figures. Do not use the “16” in a plan title as permission to buy sixteen birds.

If daily outdoor access cannot be maintained, the normal indoor example is the wrong comparison: sixteen times OSU's 8–10 square feet gives 128–160 square feet indoors. Do not try to fix that mismatch by adding more nests or longer perches.
