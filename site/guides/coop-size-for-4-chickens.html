What size chicken coop for 4 chickens?

Planning 4 hens? Compare 16 sq ft indoors, 40 sq ft run space, 1 nest boxes, 3 ft roost and sample footprints.

Quick answer for 4 standard hens

Using CoopCheck’s illustrative 4/10 area targets, start with 16 sq ft of usable indoor coop floor and 40 sq ft of secure outdoor run. One possible pair of independent rectangles is a 4 × 4 ft coop alongside a 5 × 8 ft run. That is a starting sketch, not an engineered plan or the only acceptable dimensions.

Small backyard with four hens

A four-bird flock can start from a compact 4 × 4 ft indoor reference rectangle, but a four-foot-wide square is not a promise that a human can stand inside it. Prioritize a door that lets you clean the entire floor.

If winter lockup is common, consider a 4 × 6 ft indoor footprint (24 sq ft) instead of the 16-sq-ft planning target.

Check whether the enclosure and the connected run leave room for a door to swing, a secure latch and access to each nesting box.

How those numbers are calculated

4 × 4 = 16 sq ft of indoor space; 4 × 10 = 40 sq ft of outdoor run. Nest boxes: ceil(4 ÷ 4) = 1. Roost: 4 × 9 inches = 3 ft of combined usable perch.

Do not count a wire-covered run as indoor shelter; if you have large breeds, harsh weather or regular indoor confinement, treat this as a lower planning starting point and increase the sheltered area.

Before deciding on a footprint

  1. Measure the actual usable indoor floor, not the outer roof, ramp or nesting-box projections.
  2. Check available roofed shelter, ventilation without drafts, predator entry points and cleaning access.
  3. Check the run independently for drainage, fencing, covered protection and reachability.
  4. Look up the actual city’s flock limits, setbacks and structure-permit requirements. CoopCheck does not certify a parcel.

How to read these numbers: CoopCheck uses 4 sq ft of indoor floor and 10 sq ft of outdoor run per standard laying hen as illustrative, comparatively generous planning targets for birds with daily run access. Oregon State University Extension gives at least 3 sq ft of indoor space per bird when outdoor access is available; Penn State Extension provides different poultry minimums by breed and management. The 4/10 calculation is not a universal husbandry minimum, municipal code or permit clearance. Increase room for confinement, heavy breeds, climate and flock behavior. Measure usable interior area, not roof overhangs.

Decision example: compact four-hen shelter or winter-ready shelter?

Imagine a small urban garden where the available dry indoor footprint is 4 × 4 feet. That yields 16 square feet in the example calculation, but the keeper needs to remove damp bedding through a side hatch. Before selecting a design, measure the hatch opening, check whether a long-handled scraper reaches the back corner, and place the nest entrance away from the roost droppings area. A four-foot-square structure with a tiny access door may be harder to maintain than its area figure suggests.

If the birds spend several winter days confined, compare an expanded 4 × 6-foot shelter: the extra 8 square feet do not automatically authorize more birds, but can give the existing four birds additional movement and keeper access. Draw the 40-square-foot outdoor run independently; the run and shelter must not be double-counted. Compare roof drainage and predator-proof ventilation as separate design decisions.
