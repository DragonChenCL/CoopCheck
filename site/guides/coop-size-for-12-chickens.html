What size chicken coop for 12 chickens?

Planning 12 hens? Compare 48 sq ft indoors, 120 sq ft run space, 3 nest boxes, 9 ft roost and sample footprints.

Quick answer for 12 standard hens

Using CoopCheck’s illustrative 4/10 area targets, start with 48 sq ft of usable indoor coop floor and 120 sq ft of secure outdoor run. One possible pair of independent rectangles is a 6 × 8 ft coop alongside a 10 × 12 ft run. That is a starting sketch, not an engineered plan or the only acceptable dimensions.

A twelve-hen flock is not a tiny shed

A 6 × 8 ft indoor concept covers 48 sq ft. Think about how you will clean the floor, reach each nest and avoid crowding the entry rather than treating the area calculation as a complete build specification.

Three nesting boxes and nine feet of combined roost length are calculation starting points; separate multiple bars properly and check breed-specific foot and roost needs.

The 10 × 12 ft run supplies 120 sq ft. Plot the site before building to see whether water drainage and separation from adjacent structures change the footprint.

How those numbers are calculated

12 × 4 = 48 sq ft of indoor space; 12 × 10 = 120 sq ft of outdoor run. Nest boxes: ceil(12 ÷ 4) = 3. Roost: 12 × 9 inches = 9 ft of combined usable perch.

Do not count a wire-covered run as indoor shelter; if you have large breeds, harsh weather or regular indoor confinement, treat this as a lower planning starting point and increase the sheltered area.

Before deciding on a footprint

  1. Measure the actual usable indoor floor, not the outer roof, ramp or nesting-box projections.
  2. Check available roofed shelter, ventilation without drafts, predator entry points and cleaning access.
  3. Check the run independently for drainage, fencing, covered protection and reachability.
  4. Look up the actual city’s flock limits, setbacks and structure-permit requirements. CoopCheck does not certify a parcel.

How to read these numbers: CoopCheck uses 4 sq ft of indoor floor and 10 sq ft of outdoor run per standard laying hen as illustrative, comparatively generous planning targets for birds with daily run access. Oregon State University Extension gives at least 3 sq ft of indoor space per bird when outdoor access is available; Penn State Extension provides different poultry minimums by breed and management. The 4/10 calculation is not a universal husbandry minimum, municipal code or permit clearance. Increase room for confinement, heavy breeds, climate and flock behavior. Measure usable interior area, not roof overhangs.
