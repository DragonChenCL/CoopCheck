A clear 4 x 8 coop: compare six hens with eight

A marketed overall footprint may include the outdoor run. Compare six versus eight hens in a measured 32 sq ft interior, with separate run and perch needs.

First establish the measurement

Advertised exterior dimensions are not usable indoor dimensions. This comparison assumes a verified 32 sq ft clear enclosed interior, not a 4 x 8 retail package that includes a wire run.

The same room, two different decisions

FlockIndoor area per henIndependent run referenceCombined perch reference

Both rows assume daily run access. The lower flock leaves eight square feet above its 24-square-foot indoor comparison, whereas eight hens use the full 32. This arithmetic does not assess bird compatibility, climate or the number allowed at your address.

The run can be the limiting purchase

A separate 6 x 10 ft run reaches 60 square feet, not 80. Increasing the indoor room will not change that outdoor measurement. An 8 x 10 ft run reaches the eight-hen reference only if the measured ground is genuinely usable.

Put both rectangles on a lot sketch with the gate and required distances. The sum of their areas is not a guarantee that their shapes fit around a house or setback zone. Keep access for cleaning and opening panels visible on the plan.

Ask the seller for usable dimensions

Request the clear indoor sleeping-house length and width, a fixture diagram and the attached run's dimensions separately. Ask whether the advertised bird count describes overnight roosting or a full housing scenario. Record the answer rather than treating a product title as measurement evidence.

A dated seller specification is useful but is not our independent measurement. Compare it in the model-specific buyer checker; unsupported dimensions should remain blank.

Use the comparison to decide what to verify next

Choose a tentative flock, then check the actual local allowance. Have a no-outdoor-access contingency assessed separately; the four-square-foot figure is not the source's indoor-only recommendation. If fixture loss, access or daily outdoor use is uncertain, keep the decision open instead of rounding an advertised capacity upward.
