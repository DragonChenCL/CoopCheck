Eight hens and a 4 x 8 listing: measure what is really inside

Work through exterior-versus-interior dimensions for an eight-hen coop. Includes a wall-thickness example, seller questions and separate outdoor-space check.

The phrase “4 x 8 coop” can describe a clear interior, an outside shell or a coop-and-run package. Those are different measurements and can produce different buying decisions.

Why the units matter before the bird count

Eight adult layers produce a 32 sq ft indoor comparison at four square feet each. That is exactly a clear 4 x 8 ft interior, with no inaccessible floor deducted. But converting a 48 x 96 inch outside shell to inside dimensions requires knowing the actual wall assembly.

In a deliberately hypothetical example, each opposing wall consumes three inches. The inside is then 42 x 90 inches: 42 x 90 / 144 = 26.25 sq ft. That is 5.75 sq ft below the 32-square-foot comparison. Three inches is an example input, not an assertion about any product or recommended construction.

Place six feet of perch, not just a larger number on the drawing

The selected nine-inch perch allowance totals 6 ft for eight hens. A room only four feet wide cannot provide that total with a single unobstructed four-foot bar. Two three-foot usable bars add up arithmetically, but clearance, approach and safe dismounting must be assessed separately. The worksheet does not design structural supports or prescribe bar spacing for every breed.

Round the one-nest-per-four ratio to two boxes. Check whether their footprints or storage beneath them permanently remove usable ground; avoid counting the same area twice.

Keep the 80-square-foot run on a separate line

The outdoor planning target is 80 sq ft. A measured 8 x 10 ft usable enclosure reaches it. If the “4 x 8” purchase already includes the outdoor run, do not also describe that whole rectangle as indoor floor. Request two sets of dimensions and use the buyer checker only after both are understood.

For no-outdoor-access management, eight times OSU's 8–10 sq ft range produces 64–80 sq ft indoors. That is a different scenario, not a reason to label the 32-square-foot shelter suitable for permanent confinement.

How much floor can walls remove from a nominal 4 x 8 shell?

For a nominal 48 x 96 inch EXTERIOR rectangle, subtract twice the wall assembly thickness on each axis before converting to square feet. This is a geometry sensitivity test, not a measured wall specification for any vendor. The resulting enclosed rectangular floor still needs to be checked for fixed obstructions, usable clearance and roost access.

Calculated examples: (48 - 2t) x (96 - 2t) / 144. No inner fixtures are deducted.

That is a planning preference, not a local ordinance or a guarantee. OSU gives at least 3 sq ft per bird with outdoor access, or 24 sq ft for eight hens, and 8-10 sq ft per bird if birds lack outdoor access. Under the example 4-inch wall scenario, the 24.44 sq ft rectangle would be close to the 24 sq ft comparison before any obstructions, but still below our 32 sq ft plan target. Purchaser action: request clear inside dimensions, whether floor under external nest boxes is counted, where the door swings, and the net floor available when fittings are installed. If only an exterior kit size is published, postpone assigning a flock count. See named-product evidence examples and draw the 80-square-foot run separately.
