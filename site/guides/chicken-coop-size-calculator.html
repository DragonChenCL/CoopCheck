Indoor coop vs. chicken run: two different jobs

The coop is where birds sleep and shelter from bad weather. The run allows movement, dust bathing and outdoor activity. A 24-sq-ft coop with a separate 60-sq-ft run is not an 84-sq-ft coop.

When evaluating ready-made products, confirm net usable indoor floor area. Outdoor mesh space should not be counted as indoor shelter. Penn State Extension specifically warns against advertised capacity based on the outdoor portion.

How to calculate nest boxes and roost length

Estimate one 12-inch-square nest box per four adult hens (round up); plan 9 inches of combined roost per bird. OSU Extension recommends about one nest per four and describes 5–10 inches of roost per bird. The location and shape of the components still need a real design check.

For six birds: ceil(6 ÷ 4) = two nesting boxes; 6 × 9 in = 54 in, or 4.5 ft of total roost.

Can a coop fit your backyard?

A geometry result alone is not enough. First calculate separate coop/run spaces, then sketch them on a measured property plan. Check actual city animal limits and whether rules measure to the property line or to a neighboring home. Your city’s rules and a suitable buildable area must be verified independently.

Coop space and city chicken limits are different checks

This calculator estimates usable indoor coop area and a separate run for your flock; it does not establish how many birds your address may legally keep or where the enclosure may sit. For a concrete example, Portland’s flock allowance depends on lot size, while nearby Oregon cities use different flock and permit rules. Check the applicable city law first, then use the property-line setback worksheet to understand which distance your local ordinance actually measures.

Worked example: a Portland lot with four adult hens

For four standard hens, CoopCheck estimates 16 sq ft of usable indoor floor plus a separate 40 sq ft outdoor run. Portland §13.40.040(A) instead mandates at least 40 sq ft of usable shelter or pen area for four adult fowl. This does not mean Portland legally requires both our 16-sq-ft indoor target and a separate 40-sq-ft run: our figures are husbandry planning targets, not a city-law minimum.

On a hypothetical 5,000-sq-ft Portland lot, the ordinary flock limit is four; for an otherwise qualifying 12,000-sq-ft lot it is six. Your coop still must satisfy local structure setbacks and other applicable property rules. After calculating the housing sizes, try the free four-hen concept layout.
