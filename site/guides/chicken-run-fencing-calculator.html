Example: 8 × 12 ft run with 6-ft mesh walls

Perimeter = 2 × (8 + 12) = 40 linear ft. Wall mesh (no doors subtracted) = 40 × 6 = 240 sq ft. Optional mesh roof = 8 × 12 = 96 sq ft. A 1-ft-wide continuous apron adds 40 × 1 = 40 sq ft before corner overlaps, cutting waste and seams: total simple surface area 376 sq ft.

Actual mesh roll selection requires overlap, fastener details and specification review. A shared wall with a solid coop changes the geometry.

What this estimate does not include

It does not choose wire gauge, opening size, buried fence depth, roof support, post spacing, footing, doors, latches or labor. Oregon State University Extension recommends overhead covering and buried protective fencing to reduce predator access, but the right barrier depends on the site and local wildlife. Confirm any code rules for fences and animal structures too.

A mesh area number is not permission to skip engineering or supplier-specific material requirements.

Turn mesh area into a materials enquiry without inventing a roll count

The calculator returns geometric area, not a shopping list. The same 96-square-foot run can require different amounts of vertical mesh because perimeter changes with its shape. These worked cases assume six-foot-high side walls, a fully meshed horizontal roof and a one-foot outward ground apron, all before cutting waste and door-specific details.

Why 376 ÷ 200 is not a roll purchase order

A hypothetical 4-foot-wide × 50-foot-long roll contains 200 square feet by area. The 8 × 12 example has 376 square feet of nominal mesh surfaces, so 376 ÷ 200 = 1.88 rolls by raw area alone; rounding to two is only a theoretical lower bound. A six-foot wall is taller than one four-foot strip. Joining strips requires an approved overlap and attachment method, and roof sections and gate cuts constrain how lengths can be cut. The minimum area calculation cannot tell you how many rolls to buy.

Do not confuse a horizontal apron with a buried vertical barrier

An outward apron and a vertical buried extension have different placement and protection details even if their rectangular mesh areas are numerically equal. Penn State Extension discusses burying wire at least 12 inches and toeing it outward around six inches; UConn Extension describes another buried L/J configuration. These are educational practices, not guaranteed predator-proof designs or rules for every location.

Ask the supplier for: roll width and length; actual mesh opening and wire gauge; overlap/fastening instructions; compatible roof mesh; gate and corners; ground conditions and local predator exposure. Keep each measurement in feet and each area in square feet; never add them directly.

Turn area into a purchase quantity without hiding the omissions

For an example 8 x 12 ft run with 6 ft walls: perimeter is 40 ft; wall area is 240 sq ft; a flat mesh top adds 96. The calculator's optional one-foot apron adds perimeter x width = 40 sq ft, producing 376 sq ft in that scenario.

Important limitation: perimeter x apron width represents strips along the sides. A fully filled rectangular outer band would also include corner squares: (8 + 2) x (12 + 2) − 8 x 12 = 44 sq ft. The four extra square feet are not included in the strip estimate. Your chosen corner layout, overlaps, cut-offs and attachment method change the material order.

For a hypothetical 4 x 50 ft roll, 376 / 200 rounds up to two rolls by area alone. That does not prove two rolls will fit the cut layout or provide the correct wire strength, opening size or predator protection. Draw panel cuts at the actual roll width before ordering. This tool does not engineer posts, roof supports, gates or underground installation.
