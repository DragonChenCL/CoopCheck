Copy-ready dimensions request to send a seller

Before ordering, please confirm the unobstructed internal floor length and width of the sleeping house (excluding nest boxes and built-in fixtures); the separately enclosed run's usable dimensions and whether it is included; total roost-bar length; and whether the advertised hen count describes roosting positions or a housing recommendation for birds with regular outdoor access. If there is a current technical drawing or specification sheet for this exact model, please share it.

The answer may be “not available.” That is a meaningful information gap, not permission to treat the external product dimensions as usable indoor space.

Six questions to answer before ordering a chicken coop

  • What is the exact clear sleeping-floor area? Request inside dimensions after nesting boxes, built-in fixtures and unusable spaces.
  • What is the separate enclosed run area? Ask whether it comes with the purchase, and whether that run is secure and usable.
  • What does the claimed bird capacity mean? “Roosting spots,” “suitable for,” and tested daily housing capacity are different statements.
  • What can you clean and access? Check nest access, litter management, openings, and the ability to inspect all areas without disturbing the birds.
  • Which hazards remain untested? Ventilation, thermal conditions, weather loads, predators and the needs of larger breeds require separate evaluation.
  • Can you keep the birds at your address? City and county rules, rooster restrictions, HOA requirements, setbacks and structure permits may differ. Start with CoopCheck's city directory and verify applicable official text.

The “4 × 8 coop” trap: measure what is actually indoors

A product advertised as a 4 ft × 8 ft chicken coop has a nominal overall footprint of 32 sq ft. If the attached outdoor run occupies 6 ft of that length and a sleeping compartment takes the remaining 2 ft, the sleeping compartment's theoretical floor footprint would be only 4 × 2 = 8 sq ft before subtracting walls, nest boxes or inaccessible areas. These are illustrative numbers, not measurements of any listed brand or model.

For six standard hens, the OSU Extension's indoor-space starting reference of 3 sq ft per bird gives 18 sq ft for comparison. An 8-sq-ft theoretical sleeping floor is therefore not interchangeable with a 32-sq-ft advertised overall footprint; it does not meet that single reference for six birds. This comparison alone still does not determine ventilation, welfare, winter confinement or legal capacity.

  1. Measure inside the sleeping house, not its exterior roof or roof overhang.
  2. Calculate separately: clear indoor length × clear indoor width, and usable run length × run width.
  3. Ask the seller which compartments, nesting boxes and fixed structures their advertised dimensions include.
  4. Enter your verified measurements into the free indoor-versus-run checker, then verify city siting and permit rules before buying.

Important: never substitute a hypothetical layout for the manufacturer's technical drawing. Missing interior measurements should remain marked unknown rather than reverse-engineered from the marketing photo.

DIY or prefab? Compare the full project, not just the advertised coop price

A prefab coop can look cheaper because its advertised price may cover only the small sleeping house. A DIY estimate can look cheaper when tools, the enclosure, and ongoing maintenance are missing. Neither conclusion is reliable until the scope of both quotes is the same.
