What size chicken coop for 6 chickens?

Planning 6 hens? Compare 24 sq ft indoors, 60 sq ft run space, 2 nest boxes, 4.5 ft roost and sample footprints.

Quick answer for 6 standard hens

Using CoopCheck’s illustrative 4/10 area targets, start with 24 sq ft of usable indoor coop floor and 60 sq ft of secure outdoor run. One possible pair of independent rectangles is a 4 × 6 ft coop alongside a 6 × 10 ft run. That is a starting sketch, not an engineered plan or the only acceptable dimensions.

A six-hen coop with a separate run

A 4 × 6 ft indoor rectangle supplies 24 sq ft, while a 6 × 10 ft run supplies 60 sq ft. Keep those as separate areas: a small shelter attached to a mesh run is not a 84-sq-ft indoor coop.

Six hens need two nest boxes on this starting rule. The boxes should remain accessible without walking through the roost or droppings area.

When planning a 6 × 10 ft run, reserve a clear route for wheelbarrow access and for reaching corners during cleaning.

How those numbers are calculated

6 × 4 = 24 sq ft of indoor space; 6 × 10 = 60 sq ft of outdoor run. Nest boxes: ceil(6 ÷ 4) = 2. Roost: 6 × 9 inches = 4.5 ft of combined usable perch.

Do not count a wire-covered run as indoor shelter; if you have large breeds, harsh weather or regular indoor confinement, treat this as a lower planning starting point and increase the sheltered area.

Before deciding on a footprint

  1. Measure the actual usable indoor floor, not the outer roof, ramp or nesting-box projections.
  2. Check available roofed shelter, ventilation without drafts, predator entry points and cleaning access.
  3. Check the run independently for drainage, fencing, covered protection and reachability.
  4. Look up the actual city’s flock limits, setbacks and structure-permit requirements. CoopCheck does not certify a parcel.

How to read these numbers: CoopCheck uses 4 sq ft of indoor floor and 10 sq ft of outdoor run per standard laying hen as illustrative, comparatively generous planning targets for birds with daily run access. Oregon State University Extension gives at least 3 sq ft of indoor space per bird when outdoor access is available; Penn State Extension provides different poultry minimums by breed and management. The 4/10 calculation is not a universal husbandry minimum, municipal code or permit clearance. Increase room for confinement, heavy breeds, climate and flock behavior. Measure usable interior area, not roof overhangs.

Decision example: where do six hens, two nests and a cleanout door fit?

A 4 × 6-foot shelter is a 24-square-foot indoor reference, but that number alone does not position the door, roost or nests. Sketch the full opening arc of a cleanout hatch first. Next reserve a route to both suggested nest boxes that avoids placing them directly beneath the perches. The six-hen roost calculation is a combined 4.5 feet of usable bar: check whether separated bars have enough room to keep birds from rubbing on walls or each other.

The matching 6 × 10-foot outdoor run reaches the example 60-square-foot target only while all of that floor remains usable. If a feed bin, rain shelter or permanently inaccessible corner occupies part of the run, recalculate the remaining clear area. The six-hen case illustrates a design tradeoff that a simple flock-count multiplication cannot show: space for chores and safe circulation competes with the nominal footprint.
