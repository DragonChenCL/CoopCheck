Why city-level rules matter

Backyard chicken questions are usually local: one city may limit flock size, another may require a permit, and another may measure coop separation from a neighboring dwelling instead of the property line. CoopCheck keeps the city rule and the map-based geometry check separate so the tool does not pretend to verify rules it cannot yet calculate.

All 14 Texas city profiles

13 profiles include some official-source research; 1 remain research pending and are not eligible for indexing. The notes below are starting points, not a statewide permission or address-level legal determination. Open the city page for current citations, exceptions and review scope.

  • Arlington, TX

    Source reviewed

    Up to 4 chickens without distance or lot-size restrictions; larger flocks have extra rules

  • Austin, TX

    Limited-scope source review

    Austin CURRENT Chapter 3 §§3-2-11,3-2-16 governs covered four-sided housing and enclosure geometry but sets NO SINGLE NUMERIC HEN CAP in those clauses: housing must be adequately sized for species and number. This is NOT an unlimited-hen entitlement; zoning, occupancy, nuisances and property restrictions still require review.

  • Dallas, TX

    Limited-scope source review

    No universal number of backyard hens established from this cited code excerpt

  • Denton, TX

    Source reviewed

    No more than 8 hens per residential parcel

  • El Paso, TX

    Limited-scope source review

    El Paso §7.24.020(B)(1): fewer than SIX fowl/rabbits as HOUSEHOLD PETS qualify for the specific poultry permit EXCEPTION. SIX OR MORE normally requires poultry permit outside permitted ranch-farm zones. The city does NOT set a universal max of six in this clause; permitted total decided by §7.24.050 veterinary officer and zoning.

  • Fort Worth, TX

    Source reviewed

    Up to 12 fowl on lots ½ acre or less; higher limits on larger lots

  • Frisco, TX

    Limited-scope source review

    No more than 12 gallinaceous fowl in one enclosure under §14-17

  • Garland, TX

    Limited-scope source review

    Up to 4 female domestic fowl over 1 month old, across domestic fowl types

  • Houston, TX

    Limited-scope source review

    Houston Code §6-35 sets up to 30 of one listed fowl/animal type and up to 40 combined on a 65×125-ft or smaller enclosure, with larger-enclosure ratio adjustment; the 100-ft nearby-building restriction and §6-39 special exception still apply. Not a blanket residential entitlement.

  • Irving, TX

    Source reviewed

    Up to 25 chickens on parcels <=1 acre; larger-parcel rooster-to-hen ratio rules apply

  • McKinney, TX

    Source reviewed

    Up to 12 hens at a single detached residential home

  • Plano, TX

    Limited-scope source review

    Plano §4-185 permits backyard HENS at property with one lawful dwelling but does not declare an unconditional flock-specific cap. General adult-animal limits §4-81 (usually 10 at a qualifying one-dwelling property) and zoning apply; not an unlimited-hen approval.

  • Round Rock, TX

    Research pending — noindex

    City-specific rules have not been verified. Do not use this profile as approval to keep poultry or build a coop.

  • San Antonio, TX

    Source reviewed

    Up to 8 domestic fowl per residence, including at most 1 rooster, without an excess-animal permit
