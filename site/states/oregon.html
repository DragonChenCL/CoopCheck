How many chickens can you have in Oregon city limits?

There is no single chicken limit across the Oregon cities CoopCheck covers. Portland, Eugene, Salem and Gresham each use different flock-size, rooster, permit and coop-placement rules.

Quick answer

Chicken limits inside Oregon city limits vary by city.

For the four Oregon cities currently covered by CoopCheck, the headline limits range from 3 adult hens in Gresham to 12 poultry in Salem. Portland uses a lot-size-based limit, while Eugene separately counts adults and younger birds.

Portland4 on any lot; 6 on lots ≥10,000 sq ft
EugeneUp to 6 adults + 6 under 6 months on lots under 20,000 sq ft
SalemUp to 12 poultry total per property
GreshamUp to 3 adult hens on eligible single-family property; permit required

Common questions

Oregon city-limit chicken FAQs

How many chickens can you have in the city limits in Oregon?

It depends on the city. Among the Oregon cities CoopCheck currently covers, Portland allows 4 on any lot and 6 on lots of 10,000 square feet or more; Eugene allows up to 6 adults plus 6 under 6 months on lots under 20,000 square feet; Salem allows up to 12 poultry; and Gresham allows up to 3 adult hens on eligible single-family property with a permit.

Can you have chickens inside Oregon city limits?

Yes in Portland, Eugene, Salem and Gresham, subject to each city’s own rules. Rooster restrictions, permits, setbacks and property-specific zoning can still change what is allowed at a particular address.

Do you need a permit for backyard chickens in Oregon cities?

It varies. Gresham requires a chicken permit, while Portland does not require a standard livestock permit. Use the city page and linked official source for the jurisdiction you are checking.

Why city-level rules matter

Backyard chicken questions are usually local: one city may limit flock size, another may require a permit, and another may measure coop separation from a neighboring dwelling instead of the property line. CoopCheck keeps the city rule and the map-based geometry check separate so the tool does not pretend to verify rules it cannot yet calculate.
