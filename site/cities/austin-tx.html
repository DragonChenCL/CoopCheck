Flock size

Primary clause recorded

Austin CURRENT Chapter 3 §§3-2-11,3-2-16 governs covered four-sided housing and enclosure geometry but sets NO SINGLE NUMERIC HEN CAP in those clauses: housing must be adequately sized for species and number. This is NOT an unlimited-hen entitlement; zoning, occupancy, nuisances and property restrictions still require review.

Austin Ch3 §§3-2-1, 3-2-11, 3-2-16(A)-(E) current Oct2026 · recorded 2026-10-13

What to confirm for this proposal

Ask which zoning, nuisance and site constraints limit this proposal. A missing universal number is not permission for an unlimited flock.

Roosters

Primary clause recorded

Austin §3-2-2 bars keeping ANY ANIMAL making frequent, long, continued noise disturbing normal residents, applicable to roosters; §3-2-1 prohibits fowl roaming at large. The examined Chapter3 animal rules do NOT independently establish a CITYWIDE CATEGORICAL ROOSTER BAN or an unconditional right to keep them.

Austin Code §§3-2-2 Noisy Animals, 3-2-1 Running at Large; §3-2-16 · recorded 2026-10-13

What to confirm for this proposal

Confirm the applicable noise and nuisance conditions; a conditional statement is not an unconditional rooster allowance.

Permits and approvals

Primary clause recorded

Austin City Code §§3-2-11 and 3-2-16 affirmatively REQUIRE any chicken enclosure to have four secure sides, overhead cover, adequate size, sanitation and comply with fence-height/setback rules. This legal enclosure standard does NOT independently decide whether a structure BUILDING permit or separate keeping license is needed on the parcel.

Austin Code §§3-2-11(A)-(B), 3-2-16(A)-(E), city Public Health Animal Pens guidance · recorded 2026-10-13

What to confirm for this proposal

Request the keeping/licensing procedure and renewal conditions, then ask separately about the proposed structure, utilities and building approvals.

Coop placement

Primary clause recorded

Current Austin City Code §3-2-16(A)-(E) applies to ANY fowl enclosure, not just 2+ chickens: secure four-sided covered coop, at least 30 ft from adjacent residence/business building other than keeper's own. If birds/coop create a HEALTH HAZARD, at least 50 ft. These are building separations, not property-line offsets; fences and local building/zoning rules separately apply.

Austin Municipal Code §§3-2-11(A)-(B), 3-2-16(A)-(E), amended Ord.20221013-030 effective 2022-10-24 · recorded 2026-10-13

What to confirm for this proposal

Label both boundary and building distances separately. Ask which rule applies to the coop, the run or the whole enclosure and how each distance is measured.

Which Austin chicken rules apply to your yard?

Current Austin Code §3-2-16 applies to all fowl enclosures, even for a single bird, with a normal 30-ft rule and a 50-ft health-hazard rule. It does not provide one universal hen cap, rooster decision or permit clearance.

Examples: what the numbers mean

  • Three chickens, coop 18 ft from the neighbor's house

    This is short of Austin's 30-ft minimum for any fowl enclosure. Review a different placement.

  • Three chickens, coop 35 ft from the neighboring home

    This exceeds the ordinary 30-ft distance but not the 50-ft health-hazard rule or any separate flock-size, zoning and permit requirements.

  • Coop 30 ft from the fence but 15 ft from a business

    Austin §3-2-16 measures to the neighboring residence/business building (30 ft normally, 50 ft with a health hazard). Measuring only to a fence is insufficient.

Before you plan a coop

  1. Confirm the property is inside Austin city limits; check zoning, subdivision restrictions and any HOA conditions.
  2. Locate nearby residential and business structures and draw the coop-to-structure distance.
  3. Ask the city to verify the actual flock cap, rooster rule, permitting and building requirements before buying birds.
