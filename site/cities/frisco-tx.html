Coop placement

Primary clause recorded

Frisco §§14-17(b),14-18 require clean, secure, sanitary gallinaceous-fowl enclosures that avoid nuisances. These poultry provisions do NOT supply a universal numeric coop setback. §14-16(d)'s 100-ft rule concerns small/large LIVESTOCK, and should not automatically be transferred to fowl; verify zoning/building conditions separately.

Frisco Code §§14-17(b), 14-18(a)-(b) (fowl enclosure and sanitation); compare §14-16(d) livestock-specific separation · recorded 2026-10-13

What was reviewed — and what is not confirmed?

These field notes reflect the linked sources, but not every rule or exception has been confirmed against the currently effective code. See the evidence audit.

Current Frisco Chapter 14 §§14-17, 14-18: at most 12 gallinaceous fowl in one combined enclosure, qualified >=1-acre defense for male fowl, mandatory enclosure sanitation. §14-16 livestock 100-ft proximity rule is not a proven poultry coop setback; building/zoning and poultry licensing separately unresolved.

A twelve-bird enclosure cap is not a property-wide guarantee

Frisco's cited ordinance applies a cap of twelve gallinaceous birds in one enclosure; that wording should not be presented as unconditional permission to keep twelve hens on any residential lot. Its rooster clause includes a conditional affirmative defense for premises of at least one acre, which is not a general exemption from nuisance and zoning standards.

Frisco's fowl provisions emphasize enclosed, sanitary housing, but the separate 100-foot rule in the livestock section cannot automatically be transferred to chicken coops. Confirm the operative land-use and structure provisions instead of drawing an invented universal hundred-foot buffer in the planner.
