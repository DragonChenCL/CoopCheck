How many chickens can you have in Portland, Oregon?

Inside Portland city limits, you can keep up to 4 chickens or other similarly sized domestic fowl on any lot, or up to 6 on lots of 10,000 square feet or more. No standard livestock permit is required, but rooster and coop-placement rules still apply.

Quick answer

Most Portland lots can keep up to 4 chickens.

Portland City Code allows up to 4 small domestic fowl on any lot and up to 6 on lots of 10,000 square feet or more. Up to four additional young fowl under 12 weeks are allowed. Roosters are generally prohibited unless the property qualifies for agricultural use.

Portland also has separate rules for lots 20,000 square feet or larger that allow agricultural uses, so check the official code if that applies to your property.

Can you have chickens inside Portland city limits?

Yes. Portland allows backyard chickens inside city limits, but the flock-size rule is only one layer. Roosters, enclosure standards, nuisance rules, property-line setbacks, building requirements and zoning can still affect a specific property.

Common questions

How many chickens can you have in Portland, Oregon?

Up to four chickens and other similarly sized domestic fowl are allowed on any lot, and up to six are allowed on lots of 10,000 square feet or more. Portland also allows up to four additional young fowl under 12 weeks of age.

Can you have chickens inside Portland city limits?

Yes. Portland allows backyard chickens subject to quantity, enclosure, nuisance and placement standards in Title 13.

Are roosters allowed in Portland?

Roosters are generally prohibited except for agricultural purposes on lots that allow agricultural uses.

Do I need a chicken permit in Portland?

No standard livestock permit is required, but Title 13 and other building or zoning rules still apply.

What are Portland's chicken coop setbacks?

Livestock facility structures must be at least 3 feet from side and rear property lines and 10 feet from the front property line.
