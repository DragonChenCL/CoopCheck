Can you keep chickens in Arlington?

Yes. Arlington permits small backyard flocks and adds distance and lot-size rules as flock size increases. The exact answer for a specific home can still depend on zoning, lot size, accessory-structure rules, neighboring buildings and private HOA or CC&R restrictions.

Which Arlington chicken rules apply to your yard?

Arlington treats small flocks differently: up to four birds do not trigger the extra lot-size and distance thresholds described for larger groups. That does not remove animal confinement or building obligations.

Examples: what the numbers mean

  • Four hens on a smaller residential lot

    The published chicken-count rule does not impose a lot-size or additional dwelling-distance test at four; other rules remain.

  • Six hens on 0.4 acre, nearest dwelling 35 ft away

    The city's published 5–10-bird sub-half-acre tier specifies 50 ft, so this proposed spot falls short.

  • Exactly ten chickens or a lot at the half-acre boundary

    The city's simplified published ranges overlap at ten. Confirm the governing ordinance and how the boundary is applied.

Before you plan a coop

  1. Count all birds and check whether agricultural zoning exceptions apply to roosters.
  2. For more than four birds, verify actual acreage and measure distance to the nearest dwelling, not just to a fence.
  3. For ten-plus birds and all structural permit questions, consult Arlington's current §5.02 and Animal Services.
