Scope and limitations

Current codified Tigard Chapter 6.02 Article VII (Ord. 22-09) and City Code Compliance describe no universal numeric hen limit in the livestock-keeping article, while prohibiting roosters over 3 months old and requiring >=5-ft shelter lot-line/public-access-easement offsets, shelters at night, and nuisance controls. Check zone, lot coverage and separate construction approvals; no all-clear on unusually large flocks. 2026-10-13: Official city rule mapping for permit. Building-permit classification, animal keeping licence and parcel-level zoning must be independently established; only cited positive duties and conditional exemptions are confirmed.

Questions to check before building

How many chickens can I keep here?

The current Tigard Livestock Article VII, §§6.02.610–630, specifies NO universal numerical chicken/flock ceiling. City Code Compliance says ownership of backyard livestock/poultry is not itself regulated as a numerical limit; rooster age, coop setbacks, nighttime enclosure, sanitation/nuisance and zoning standards still apply. This is NOT an unlimited-flock approval.

Are roosters allowed?

Roosters older than 3 months prohibited

Do I need a permit?

Tigard states a detached single-story NONHABITABLE standard accessory structure ≤200 SQ FT and ≤15ft roof-height does NOT need a building permit, subject to structural/use conditions. Larger or taller structures may require one. Front setbacks and maximum accessory area apply EVEN IF PERMIT EXEMPT; a specific chicken coop and any poultry-keeping license require city confirmation.

Where can the coop be placed?

Coops at least 5 ft from all property lines (fenced runs exempt); animals must be enclosed at night

Before buying birds or constructing a coop

Confirm that your address is inside the correct city jurisdiction; look up your parcel zoning; confirm flock-size and rooster restrictions, any poultry permit, neighboring-dwelling or property-line separation, accessory-structure/building permits, and HOA or deed restrictions. City rules can change.
