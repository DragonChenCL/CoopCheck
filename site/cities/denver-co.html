Common questions

How many chickens can I keep in Denver?

Up to eight hens or ducks, or a combination not exceeding eight, under the Food-Producing Animals program.

Are roosters allowed in Denver?

No. The FPA permit covers hens but not roosters.

Do I need a permit for backyard chickens in Denver?

Yes. Denver lists a one-time $25 Food-Producing Animals permit per property.

Which Denver chicken rules apply to your yard?

Denver's Food-Producing Animals (FPA) program combines an eight-bird ceiling with a permit and a separate area/placement condition from its city-issued information packet.

Examples: what the numbers mean

  • Six hens and two ducks

    Eight birds meets the basic count cap. The packet calls for at least 128 sq ft permeable space, with all other restrictions still checked.

  • Seven hens and two ducks

    Nine birds exceeds the FPA count cap.

  • Eight birds on concrete in the front yard

    The bird count alone is not enough; the packet calls for permeable space in the rear half of the lot.

Before you plan a coop

  1. Count hens and ducks together and exclude roosters/drakes from the proposed flock.
  2. Outline the rear half of the zone lot and estimate 16 sq ft of permeable area multiplied by the bird count.
  3. Contact Denver Zoning for structure setbacks/permit specifics, then follow the FPA permit application process.
