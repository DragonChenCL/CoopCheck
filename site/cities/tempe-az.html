Can you keep chickens in Tempe?

Yes. Tempe allows domestic chickens on residential lots with single-family dwellings, subject to its animal-quantity rules. The exact answer for a specific home can still depend on zoning, lot size, accessory-structure rules, neighboring buildings and private HOA or CC&R restrictions.

A seventh hen raises a different use-permit question

Tempe's official zoning summary allows up to six domestic chickens on eligible residential lots. Adding a seventh cannot be justified merely by providing extra floor space: the city describes a use-permit process for exceeding the normal allowance. Roosters are restricted outside agricultural zoning, so replacing a hen with a rooster also changes the result.

For a site under one acre, Tempe's published rule limits chicken enclosure height relative to the property's fence or wall. This is a height restriction, not a universal numerical lot-line setback. Record the parcel zoning, enclosure height and proposed bird count before relying on any drawing.
