Local ordinance watch · California · updated October 24, 2026

Grover Beach will discuss poultry keeping on October 26. No new allowance has been confirmed.

The city's official council agenda lists “Policy Discussion on Keeping Poultry within City Limits” as regular-business Item 5 for Monday, October 26, 2026. A discussion agenda is not an enacted ordinance.

Policy statusDiscussion pending; outcome unknown
Critical status note: As of this page's October 24 research cutoff, CoopCheck has not verified adoption of an ordinance changing the city's poultry rules. Do not treat a council discussion or temporary enforcement pause as permanent permission to keep hens or build a coop.

What the government record confirms

The official October 26 agenda names Community Development Director Martin and Code Compliance Officer Davenport as staff for the poultry policy discussion. The council meets at the Council Chamber, 154 S. 8th Street, Grover Beach. The city's calendar entry shows a 6:00 p.m. start.

New Times San Luis Obispo reported in September that council members had approved a temporary hold on enforcement of existing poultry violations on September 14 while considering future policy options. That reporting is context—not proof of a permanent exception, a new chicken cap, or the final outcome of the October 26 meeting.

Three things residents should not assume

  1. Enforcement pause does not equal repeal. A temporary stay in enforcement may be limited in scope and time. Written city clarification is essential.
  2. Permitting, rooster rules and setbacks are separate questions. Even if the council eventually changes animal-keeping permissions, the location and construction of coops might be subject to other requirements.
  3. Future approval is not a present entitlement. Until adopted law is confirmed, do not purchase birds or begin construction based on proposed policy alone.

Questions to ask before changing a property

  • Does the discussion propose permitting hens, ducks or other poultry, and does it distinguish roosters?
  • Are flock limits, lot sizes, permits or registration fees proposed?
  • Would existing owners be grandfathered, and does an enforcement pause have an expiry date?
  • Which zoning, building, enclosure setback and nuisance provisions would remain applicable?
  • If an ordinance is proposed, what are the required readings, final adoption date and effective date?
