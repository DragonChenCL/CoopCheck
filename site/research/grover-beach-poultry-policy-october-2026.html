Grover Beach chicken-ban discussion: 15-foot setback proposed, no new ordinance yet

The city's official council agenda lists “Policy Discussion on Keeping Poultry within City Limits” as regular-business Item 5 for Monday, October 26, 2026. A discussion agenda is not an enacted ordinance.

Policy statusAgenda confirmed; meeting action not verified
Critical status note: As of the October 29 follow-up, CoopCheck has verified the October 26 agenda and reviewed the October 28 KCBX coverage but has not obtained an adopted ordinance changing poultry rules. Do not treat a council discussion or temporary enforcement pause as permanent permission to keep hens or build a coop.

What the government record confirms

The official October 26 agenda names Community Development Director Martin and Code Compliance Officer Davenport as staff for the poultry policy discussion. The council meets at the Council Chamber, 154 S. 8th Street, Grover Beach. The city's calendar entry shows a 6:00 p.m. start.

New Times San Luis Obispo reported in September that council members had approved a temporary hold on enforcement of existing poultry violations on September 14 while considering future policy options. That reporting is context—not proof of a permanent exception, a new chicken cap, or the final outcome of the October 26 meeting.

October 27 update: agenda verified; council action not yet verified

CoopCheck rechecked the city's October 26 meeting archive and the SLO-SPAN meeting listings on October 27. The archive identifies poultry as Agenda Item 5 and SLO-SPAN lists the council meeting among recent meetings. Neither index alone confirms which policy direction the council chose, whether any vote occurred, or whether a new ordinance became effective. We have not verified an adopted change.

For a reliable final answer, verify the Item 5 recording or official minutes, note any motion and vote, then look for the full adopted ordinance and its effective date. Minutes may appear only after approval at a later meeting. Official meeting minutes and recordings index is the follow-up source. Until then, this page is an unresolved policy status report, not a statement that backyard poultry is newly legal.

October 28 reporting: a proposed 15-foot setback, not an enforceable new rule

KCBX reporter Kendra Hanna's October 28 account says council members supported a future policy under which poultry would be housed in coops at night at least 15 feet from the property line. KCBX reports they did not reach consensus on roosters and that an ordinance still requires a further vote. These details are attributed to KCBX reporting, not to independently authenticated final minutes.

What this means for a backyard design: a 15-foot nighttime coop placement condition, if formally adopted, could constrain small or narrow lots even where hens are eventually permitted. It should not yet be used as the operative legal setback. CoopCheck will update its guidance only after confirming the council's recorded action, any adopted ordinance text and the effective date with the city.

Source distinction: the official October 26 agenda confirms Item 5 was scheduled for discussion; the October 28 KCBX article reports the substance of the discussion. Neither is proof of a new enacted ordinance.

Three things residents should not assume

  1. Enforcement pause does not equal repeal. A temporary stay in enforcement may be limited in scope and time. Written city clarification is essential.
  2. Permitting, rooster rules and setbacks are separate questions. Even if the council eventually changes animal-keeping permissions, the location and construction of coops might be subject to other requirements.
  3. Future approval is not a present entitlement. Until adopted law is confirmed, do not purchase birds or begin construction based on proposed policy alone.

Questions to ask before changing a property

  • Does the discussion propose permitting hens, ducks or other poultry, and does it distinguish roosters?
  • Are flock limits, lot sizes, permits or registration fees proposed?
  • Would existing owners be grandfathered, and does an enforcement pause have an expiry date?
  • Which zoning, building, enclosure setback and nuisance provisions would remain applicable?
  • If an ordinance is proposed, what are the required readings, final adoption date and effective date?
